
# Individual Reflection — International NBA Players

## 1. Research Process

For my individual portion of Milestone 2, I wanted to understand how NBA-affiliated and independent media sources portray international NBA players in relation to their national identity, home-country connections, and global audiences. My work utilized 25 articles from the inherited NBA dataset, identified as `NBA001` through `NBA025`.

I began with the existing dataset, which contained article information, source URLs, and initial AI-assisted descriptions and classifications. Rather than accepting those classifications automatically, I reviewed the articles and considered whether the descriptions accurately reflected their content. Next, I created a separate individual review log to document my interpretations and any changes I made.

My review focused on two main categories: `cultural_frame`, which describes how an article portrays an international player, and `audience_evidence`, which identifies the type of evidence used to discuss fans or audiences. I also wrote evidence notes explaining the basis for my decisions.

## 2. Interpretive Decisions

One important part of this process was determining which cultural frame best represented each article. Some articles discussed more than one aspect of a player's identity, making it necessary to decide which theme was most central.

For example, I revised `NBA002` to emphasize Giannis Antetokounmpo's emotional connection to Greece and his family's citizenship. I also revised `NBA008` to highlight Luka Doncic's connection to both Slovenia and Dallas. These examples helped me recognize that international players can be portrayed through multiple forms of belonging rather than only through their countries of origin. They also showed me the importance of explaining my reasoning, which is why I included the `coding_decision` column to document any changes I made to the initial classifications.

Another important decision involved `NBA025`, which discusses the international representation of players across NBA rosters. I classified this article as `international_recognition` because listing players' nationalities does not necessarily mean the article portrays them as national representatives.

I also distinguished between different forms of audience evidence. Direct fan quotations, measured viewership data, and claims made by leagues or teams provide different kinds of support. I wanted my classifications to reflect those differences rather than treating every mention of international popularity as equally strong evidence.

## 3. Challenges and Limitations

One challenge was that several articles contained overlapping themes. For example, a player's return to their home country could involve national pride, local celebrations, and commercial promotion. I addressed this by selecting one dominant cultural frame based on the article's main emphasis and recording supporting details in my evidence notes.

Another challenge involved source accessibility. Two Sports Business Journal articles, `NBA010` and `NBA018`, may require subscriptions for other readers. I personally accessed and reviewed both articles, but I marked their source-access condition as `subscription_needed` to make this limitation clear.

The dataset also has limitations because it contains only 25 purposively selected articles. Several players appear in multiple records, and the sample does not represent all NBA media coverage. Therefore, my interpretations can help identify patterns within these articles but should not be generalized to every international NBA player or media outlet.

## 4. AI Use and Human Review

AI assisted with organizing the initial article descriptions, suggesting classifications, and drafting and refining my documentation. However, I did not treat AI-generated interpretations as automatically correct.

I personally reviewed all 25 articles and compared the initial interpretations with the source material. After completing my review, I confirmed 21 classifications and revised four: `NBA002`, `NBA008`, `NBA018`, and `NBA025`.

The review process demonstrated why human interpretation is important when working with cultural data. AI can help identify possible patterns, but concepts such as national identity, cultural belonging, and audience connection require attention to context. My final decisions reflect my own reading of the articles rather than relying entirely on AI-generated descriptions.

## 5. Remaining Uncertainties and Lessons Learned

Some uncertainty remains because cultural framing is interpretive. Another reviewer might reasonably identify a different dominant frame in an article containing several themes. I attempted to make my decisions transparent by defining each category in the codebook and documenting my reasoning in the review log.

Audience evidence is another area where caution is necessary. An article may describe enthusiastic fans or international attention without providing enough information to establish broader audience behavior. I therefore distinguished between measured data, direct quotations, organizational claims, journalistic inference, and anecdotal observations.

Overall, this project helped me understand that creating a cultural dataset involves more than collecting articles and assigning labels. It requires making interpretive decisions, documenting the evidence behind those decisions, and acknowledging the limitations of the available sources. The review log and codebook provide a record of how I approached those responsibilities.
