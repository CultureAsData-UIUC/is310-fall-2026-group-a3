# Collection notes

## Shared question

How do sports media describe the relationship between national identity and cross-border participation through athlete development, international NBA stars, and Formula 1 allegiance?

## Sampling decisions

The collection contains 25 distinct items per strand. Soccer includes overseas pathways, domestic development, national-team choices, and accounts of limited opportunities. NBA includes home-country audiences, international media, national representation, and league audience promotion. Formula 1 includes home crowds, national symbols, team attachment, and explicit tensions in allegiance. Country references alone do not establish nationalism or audience growth.

Candidates were found by pairing athlete names with development, moved, country, identity, fans, and home, including searches of U.S. Soccer, Formula 1, NBA, independent sports reporting, and FIBA. This was iterative purposive discovery, not an exhaustive or randomly sampled search. The first six pilot items were retained. Selection emphasized relevance and readable evidence, then balanced the three strands. No athlete is the focal subject of more than eight items in a strand. At least ten items per strand originate in independent reporting; a news agency article hosted on a league website remains independent reporting.

The intended source window is January 1, 2017 through October 22, 2026. The displayed source date is preserved as published, updated, or edited. Three undated items were retained because their text clearly concerns events within that period, but their exact publication dates remain unresolved. They must be omitted from date-based comparisons unless the dates are verified.

## Examples of exclusions

- The Guardian article about why German soccer wants American players was published in 2015, outside the proposed window.
- A Guardian Pulisic development profile first published in 2016 was excluded even though its later update fell within the window.
- Player profile pages were reserved for the proposed audit rather than counted as custom articles.
- Duplicate appearances of the same syndicated story or indexed URL were excluded.
- Non-English stories and videos without an inspected transcript were not added.

These examples describe the selection rules; they are not an exhaustive log of every search result.

## Two transformations

First, public cultural material was represented as a short source record: publication context, subject, URL, text scope, evidence location, and a paraphrase. This loses images, page design, spoken performance, and much surrounding context. Indexed-text records lose more context and therefore need priority review.

Second, those working records were converted to structured CSV fields and common flags. Flags can coexist, and `unclear` preserves insufficient evidence. Most new records use excerpt scopes, so an unobserved feature was not automatically coded `no`. Physical relocation was not inferred from a new club contract, citizenship choice, or international match travel. Family relocation alone was not treated as evidence that sporting culture caused a move.

## Version changes

Version 0.2 expands the six-row pilot to 75 items. The earlier `published_date` field became `source_date` with `date_basis` and `date_note`, preserving distinctions between publication and updates. `article_excerpt` was added as an inspected scope. `access_method` records original-page versus indexed-text evidence. These changes document the actual evidence available rather than pretending all sources were read in full.

## Review and reuse

Codes are initial AI-assisted judgments. Students should verify the evidence and resolve disagreements using the codebook. The corpus has many stories centered on the same events and athletes; observations are not statistically independent. Publisher and institutional origin may shape framing. The dataset cannot identify the national identity of every quoted fan, and it does not infer race or ethnicity. Country labels and multiple eligibility options are recorded only when explicit in the text.

Retain links and paraphrases rather than redistributing full copyrighted articles. Source pages may change or become inaccessible. The access date identifies when this collection was prepared, not a permanent archive or a guarantee of later link availability.
