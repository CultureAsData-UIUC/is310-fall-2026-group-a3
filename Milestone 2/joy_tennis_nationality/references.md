# References

Citation details below were checked on 2026-11-01 against the publisher's own records (Yale Law Journal PDF, Crossref and the Erasmus University repository) and Open Library. This was done through public records, not a UIUC library login. The Shachar and Jansen summaries were checked against each article's abstract. The Hutchins and Rowe note is based on catalogue descriptions, not a full read.

## 1. Shachar (2011)

Shachar, A. (2011). Picking winners: Olympic citizenship and the global race for talent. *Yale Law Journal, 120*(8), 2088–2139.

Shachar looks at countries fast tracking citizenship for elite athletes in exchange for medals and calls it a talent for citizenship trade. That's basically the Kazakhstan federation story in our data, where money and coaching get offered for a flag. Her idea gives us a name for the `opportunity_funding` and `flag_of_convenience` frames and a way to ask whether media treat the trade as normal or shady.

Checked at (abstract and page range from the PDF): https://www.yalelawjournal.org/feature/picking-winners-olympic-citizenship-and-the-global-race-for-talent-1

## 2. Jansen, Oonk & Engbersen (2018)

Jansen, J., Oonk, G., & Engbersen, G. (2018). Nationality swapping in the Olympic field: Towards the marketization of citizenship? *Citizenship Studies, 22*(5), 523–539. https://doi.org/10.1080/13621025.2018.1477921

This paper pushes back on the idea that sport citizenship is just being bought and sold. Looking at 167 athletes who competed for two different countries at the Summer Olympics, they find only a limited number got citizenship through a pure market deal (what they call jus talenti) and a lot of switches echo earlier migrations. That's a good check on our coding, since cases like Dzalamidze (Georgian father) or Golubev (trained in Italy since his teens) don't fit a simple "bought" frame, and we should watch for media flattening them into one.

Open access copy: https://repub.eur.nl/pub/109006

## 3. Hutchins & Rowe (2012)

Hutchins, B., & Rowe, D. (2012). *Sport beyond television: The internet, digital media and the rise of networked media sport.* Routledge. https://doi.org/10.4324/9780203120415

This is the group's shared framework for how sport moved from broadcast TV into networked digital media. For my strand it explains why the same switch story shows up in very different versions across wire copies, national sites, blogs and social posts, which is the spread our outlet mix is trying to capture. It also backs up treating online outlets like Sportskeeda or Tennis365 as real parts of the sports media system and not just noise.

Library record example: https://communication.unt.edu/publications/sport-beyond-television-internet-digital-media-and-rise-networked-media-sport.html
