## Group Theme

Sports becoming part of national and global culture through television and other digital media.

## Individual Ideas

### Mason: Athlete Mobility and Opportunity

How the cultural importance and visibility of sports can influence physical travel and relocation to pursue better training and opportunities, such as a U.S. soccer player living, training, or growing up in Europe.

### Drew: NBA Globalization Through Television and Digital Media

How the NBA's growth through television and digital media has helped a larger and more influential group of international players become key figures in the league.

* Prominent international stars include Nikola Jokic, Giannis Antetokounmpo, Luka Doncic, and Shai Gilgeous-Alexander.
* These players receive significant media attention in the United States while maintaining strong connections to their home countries, where they often have large fan bases. This can bring more international attention and engagement to the NBA.
* This connects to the group theme because international players, combined with TV and digital media, can generate greater interest and viewership worldwide. Fans may feel a stronger connection to the NBA when a player from their country becomes a star or a local hero in the league.

### Bob: Formula 1, National Allegiance, and Media Framing

National allegiance in Formula 1 when driver and team nationalities diverge.

* Create a media-framing dataset that tracks how national sports outlets, such as *Corriere dello Sport* in Italy, and social media responses frame each Formula 1 race weekend.
* Code whether different teams and drivers are positioned as the nation's primary story.
* Press, broadcasters, and social media reactions form national narratives about a result. Examples include Lewis Hamilton in the United Kingdom, Kimi Antonelli in Italy, and Max Verstappen in the Netherlands.
* National sporting allegiance is not singular. Driver nationality, team nationality, and manufacturer identity may conflict within one race, allowing different outlets and audiences to frame the same result differently.

## Scholarly Context

### Shared Framework: Networked Sports Media

Hutchins and Rowe (2012) argue that sport is no longer delivered only through television; websites, mobile devices, social platforms, and other digital media reshape how sport is produced and consumed.

**Relation to our project:** This provides the main framework for comparing TV, streaming, news, and social media as connected channels through which sports become embedded in national and global culture.

### Mason: Athlete Mobility and Opportunity

Velema (2021) maps the international transfer network of 1,700 football clubs and shows that club resources, risk, geography, and recruitment networks structure migration opportunities.

**Relation to Mason's project:** The article provides a structural baseline for studying travel to Europe. Mason's analysis can then examine how media visibility and the cultural prestige of European soccer shape an athlete's perception of those already unequal opportunities.

### Drew: NBA Globalization Through Media

Falcous and Maguire (2006) analyze the NBA on British television and show that a global sport is interpreted through local media frames rather than received the same way everywhere.

**Relation to Drew's project:** This supports comparing how U.S. and home-country media present international NBA stars and whether those players act as bridges between local identity and a global league.

### Bob: Formula 1, Framing, and National Identity

Schep (2023) finds that French and British newspaper coverage of Formula 1 used social and cultural frames to construct and reinforce national identity.

**Relation to Bob's project:** It directly justifies coding which driver, team, or manufacturer becomes the national story and comparing those frames across outlets and countries.

### Ethics and Representation

Slater, Burch, and Zimmerman (2023) use framing analysis to examine how Basketball Africa League broadcasts represented players and nationalities, including the possibility of Western or postcolonial bias.

**Relation to our project:** This warns us not to treat nationality, race, or fan identity as neutral categories. We should document who created each source, allow multiple identities where necessary, avoid stereotypes, and explain uncertainty in our coding.

### Methodological Use

Following the framing approaches used by Schep (2023) and Slater et al. (2023), the group will:

* Define coding categories before analysis.
* Record the outlet, platform, country, and date for every item.
* Preserve representative examples.
* Allow more than one frame when a source contains competing national or cultural meanings.
